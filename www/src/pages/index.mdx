### Flash-MSA: Accelerating Million-Token Training with Sparse Attention Kernels

#### [Submission URL](https://nanduruganesh.github.io/flash-msa/) | 39 points | by [rawsh](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=rawsh) | [5 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48884618)

**Only the proxy forward pass is quadratic in sequence length; everything else caches block indices and runs linear-time**, which is how these kernels make million-token training practical on H100/B200 without blowing up memory or registers.

What shipped: performant, open-source Minimax Sparse Attention (MSA) training kernels in CuTeDSL targeting NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell. Unlike most public sparse-attn code that focuses on inference, this implements a full forward/backward with fused gradients for proxy and main attention.

Key ideas:
- **Blockwise sparsity (128-token blocks) via proxy max-pooling**, so you cache block indices once and reuse them through backward instead of tracking per-token indices.
- **GQA-based main attention (not MLA)**, plus group-wise proxy heads that can pick different KV subsets per group — a formulation friendlier to teams that don’t train with MLA and likely more expressive than summing across the whole layer.
- **Streaming top-k in registers during proxy forward**, skipping LSE accumulation there and cheaply recomputing proxy LSE over the sparse activations in backward to reduce register/shared-mem pressure.
- **Block-sparse main attention reparameterized into varlen flash** (borrowing the MoBA trick) for an efficient forward.
- **Fused proxy+main backward** scheduled by inverting the cached mapping from queries→blocks to blocks→queries, maximizing KV block reuse; Q/K/V tiles are shrunk to fit register budgets.
- **KL loss backprop without materializing both distributions**: compute gradients atomically from p (main) and p_px (proxy) to avoid extra shared-memory traffic while remaining equivalent to the full KL term.

Caveats and context: this is an unofficial implementation built and tested on rented H100/B200s; the author references FA4, MSA inference, and Codex, and positions this as the first performant OSS training path for MSA rather than a MiniMax-endorsed release.
