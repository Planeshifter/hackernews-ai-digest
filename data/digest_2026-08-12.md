### Electricity Pricing in the Age of AI

#### [Submission URL](https://power2026.ai/) | 26 points | by [rzk](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=rzk) | [5 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49271050)

**Power, not GPUs, is emerging as the binding constraint on AI scale**, with data centers already at ~5% of US electricity use and power demand on a two‑year doubling path that could outrun total US generation by the mid‑2030s. The author, a former hedge‑fund quant who now advises on data‑center buildouts (from procuring GPUs to negotiating with coal plants), lays out a two‑part primer: how power plants and grids actually work, how data centers get developed, and how to price US power markets.

The core framework is commodity economics applied to electricity: prices clear at the marginal cost to serve the last unit of demand, enforced by physical settlement and grid operators (ISOs). A location’s price is the solution to a system balance equation — supply = demand + net exports + change in storage — under hard constraints like transmission limits and finite storage. All units clear at the same price; when prices move, they shift production, consumption, exports, and storage until the system balances.

Natural gas specifics matter for power pricing: crude is globally arbitraged, but US gas is constrained by LNG capacity, so domestic prices are shaped by what can’t be exported in the short term, which feeds directly into power costs. For AI builders, that means site viability hinges on variable power cost, interconnection and transmission constraints, and permitting as much as on hardware procurement. The second half promises trader‑level detail on pricing US power — the mechanics you need if you’re underwriting data‑center economics or hedging load — with the throughline that electricity availability and nodal pricing will set the pace of AI growth this decade.
