### Reducing Doom Loops with Final Token Preference Optimization

#### [Submission URL](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/antidoom) | 31 points | by [dataminer](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=dataminer) | [9 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48820127)

**Looping fell from 10.2% to 1.4% on hard math/coding prompts for an early LFM2.5-2.6B checkpoint after targeted “Antidoom” training, with eval scores improving as looping decreased.** Instead of repetition penalties (which can hurt quality) or costly RL with bespoke rewards, the method pinpoints the first token that starts a repeat and trains the model to prefer coherent alternatives only at that position, leaving the rest of the distribution largely untouched.

Training data is mined by generating low‑temperature completions on a loop‑elicitation mix (LiquidAI/antidoom-mix-v1.0), detecting a loop when a span repeats ≥4 times over ≥60 characters, then targeting the first token of the first repeat. For that position, the base model’s top‑k log‑prob alternatives are filtered to up to 20 plausible substitutes as chosen tokens; regularization prevents over‑suppressing frequent culprits like “Wait”, “So”, and “ the”.

FTPO (Final Token Preference Optimization), adapted from Antislop and related to DPO, is built for precise, minimal edits:
- Trains only the final token mid‑generation.
- Allows multiple chosen tokens per sample to spread probability mass.
- Uses a KL‑like loss in logit space (no softmax) to limit gradient spill into unrelated tokens.
- Two‑part regularization lets chosen/rejected logits move more relative to a reference.

The authors trace doom loops to three forces acting together: overtrained fallback tokens under uncertainty (e.g., “ the”, “ So”, “Alternatively”, “Wait”), prior‑context reinforcement that pushes loop‑token probability toward 1, and low‑temperature/greedy decoding that removes exits—still observed even at temp=0.67. The targeted, single‑position preference shaping breaks locally reinforced cycles without broad distribution drift, which is why overall evaluations improved rather than regressed.

Practitioners pushed back on the paper's framing, arguing that the "doom loops" they encounter—especially in coding agents—are typically driven by context pollution, not baseline token tendencies. When models accumulate multiple failed fix attempts or repeated, slightly altered file reads over long contexts, the duplicated text creates an attention "honeypot" that traps the model into circling the same incorrect approaches. 

Because this behavior appears tied to how attention mechanisms handle repetitive prompt histories, developers questioned whether fixing probabilities at the final token layer just shifts the looping symptom rather than addressing the root cause. To break these loops, practitioners shared architectural shims they use instead, such as automatically rolling back the session state and replacing the history of failed attempts with a single synthesized summary.

Secondary discussion focused on the underlying drivers and broader applications of the Antidoom research:
- **Quantization vs. Alignment:** One hypothesis blamed current RLHF paradigms for training models to confidently produce filler rather than admit failure. Others countered that severe looping is primarily a symptom of precision loss; users noted that unquantized or Q6 models rarely loop, but become highly repetitive at Q4, pointing to mechanical degradation rather than alignment "metapolitics."
- **Wasted Tokens:** Commenters questioned if FTPO's ability to precisely penalize specific tokens mid-generation could be generalized to target generic verbose filler, curbing test-time compute inefficiency beyond just hard loops.

### US manufacturers' energy costs soar because of AI data center demand

#### [Submission URL](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/07/us-manufacturers-energy-costs-soar-because-of-ai-data-center-demand/) | 34 points | by [doener](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=doener) | [6 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48823772)

**PJM’s capacity price jumped from $28.92/MW‑day in 2024 to $329.17/MW‑day in 2026**, a surge tied to AI data center buildouts that is landing directly on factory bills. Belden Brick in Ohio saw its monthly capacity charge rise from $1,600 to $12,000; steelmakers report tens of millions in added annual costs, with Metallus citing a 70% increase since 2024 (+$15M/year). Electricity makes up 20–40% of steelmaking costs, each electric arc furnace draws 40–200 MW, and the sector peaks around 11 GW—so price moves bite hard.

PJM now forecasts demand outstripping supply by 6.6 GW starting in 2027, which the Wall Street Journal equates to more than six nuclear plants, heightening outage risk in Rust Belt grids even as data center construction consumes ~1M tons of steel annually. The White House is courting both Big Tech and manufacturing, touting a Ratepayer Protection Pledge that lacks enforcement and backing a one‑time PJM backstop capacity auction, while new generation and transmission remain slow to materialize.

Compounding the crunch, Cleanview tracked 266 GW of U.S. power projects canceled in 2025—about a quarter of current capacity—with 93% of them clean energy; causes include federal cancellations of some wind projects, local opposition in states like Ohio and Indiana that are also chasing data centers, scarce transmission, and high interconnection costs. Manufacturers are passing through price hikes or weighing relocation, and steel executives warn of production outages if demand keeps outrunning supply—an awkward backdrop for a “Made in America” push after a loss of 83,000 manufacturing jobs in Trump’s first year back in office.

The thread revolves around skepticism that AI's touted productivity revolution justifies its astronomic energy toll. Commenters framed the current dynamic as a wealth transfer where traditional manufacturers and everyday consumers foot the bill so Big Tech can recoup its trillion-dollar infrastructure investments in the form of more expensive everyday products.

*   **The crypto parallel:** Prediction that AI data centers will become the new crypto miners, roaming globally to strip-mine cheap electricity until local grid managers and residents rebel.
*   **The green energy defense:** Pushback against promises that AI will eventually optimize clean energy grids to offset its own consumption. Commenters dismissed these academic claims as hollow marketing hype, likening it to slapping the "AI" label on standard household appliances.
